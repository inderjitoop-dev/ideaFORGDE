OUR MISSION

A Simple Act.
An Extraordinary Impact.

Blood donation is the voluntary process of giving blood so that it can be used to treat patients who need transfusions.

Donated blood and its components can help patients experiencing severe bleeding, people undergoing surgery, individuals receiving cancer treatment, and patients with certain blood disorders.

Blood cannot be manufactured for routine clinical use. Hospitals therefore rely on donations to maintain a dependable supply.

Blood Donor aims to make donation information easier to understand and help people find appropriate blood donation resources.

UNDERSTAND YOUR BLOOD

Know Your Blood Group

The ABO and Rh systems define the eight common blood groups. Compatibility matters when red blood cells are transfused.

A+

A Positive

A+ red blood cells can generally be given to A+ and AB+ recipients.

A−

A Negative

A− red blood cells can generally be given to A−, A+, AB− and AB+ recipients.

B+

B Positive

B+ red blood cells can generally be given to B+ and AB+ recipients.

B−

B Negative

B− red blood cells can generally be given to B−, B+, AB− and AB+ recipients.

AB+

AB Positive

AB+ recipients can generally receive red blood cells from all eight ABO/Rh groups.

AB−

AB Negative

AB− red blood cells can generally be given to AB− and AB+ recipients.

O+

O Positive

O+ red blood cells can generally be given to Rh-positive recipients.

O−

O Negative

O− red blood cells are commonly used in some emergencies when the patient's blood group is not yet known.

Medical note: These are general red blood cell compatibility rules. Actual transfusions require professional testing and compatibility checks. Plasma and platelet compatibility follow different rules.

A CONTINUING NEED

The Gift of Blood Must Continue.

Blood components have limited storage lives. Hospitals need a continuing supply of donations. Eligible people can ask an authorised blood donation service when they may safely donate again.

DONATE RESPONSIBLY

Before You Donate

Donor and recipient safety are essential. Eligibility must be assessed by qualified staff.

✓ Prepare for Donation

  • Meet the age, weight and health requirements.
  • Eat a balanced meal and drink enough water.
  • Bring any required identification documents.
  • Tell staff about medicines and relevant health history.
  • Answer the screening questions honestly.

! Consult Donation Staff

  • If you feel unwell or have a fever.
  • If you have low haemoglobin or anaemia.
  • If you are pregnant or recently gave birth.
  • If you recently had surgery or certain procedures.
  • If a medication or medical condition may affect eligibility.

Eligibility requirements vary. Ask an authorised blood bank for an individual assessment.
